Survey, Simulation, and Assessment of Lunar Lander Detection Methods

Ellemieke Van Kints1, Antony Gillette1, Trey Smith1, Alex Patton1, Caleb Adams1, Leo Ting1, Cole Matarazza1
1NASA Ames Research Center
Paper (PDF) Code (GitHub) Dataset

Abstract

This study identifies and evaluates candidate solutions for object and change detection on the lunar surface. With numerous upcoming missions to the Moon, there is increased demand for space situational awareness (SSA) in and around cislunar space, including a capability to detect, localize, and monitor the activity of lunar surface assets like NASA’s Human Landing System (HLS) and Commercial Lunar Payload Service (CLPS) landers. Our analysis is grounded in a design reference mission with spacecraft in a ∼100 km Low Lunar Orbit (LLO) utilizing an optic with ≤2 m effective surface resolution. We assess candidate solutions across multiple datasets, including synthetic imagery generated from our simulation framework, real imagery from the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO), and hybrid mixtures of both. To assess the feasibility of detection methods on relevant space edge computing, we define a targeted set of metrics: maximizing both precision and recall, while minimizing memory footprint and execution time. We evaluate nine change-detection algorithms on IM-1 site imagery. Finally, we recommend top candidate solutions for lunar surface object and change detection to consider for future lunar missions.
